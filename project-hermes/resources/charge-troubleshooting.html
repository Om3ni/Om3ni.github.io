    Reference

    Before you test
    1. Filters, blower and both coils clean. Dampers open.
    2. Know the metering device and the refrigerant.
    3. Set and verify indoor airflow first: nominally 400 CFM per ton. No charge reading means anything with wrong airflow.
    4. Work out the pressures the system should run at from the design temperature difference (below).
    5. Test heat pumps in cooling mode.

    8–12°F typical
    Total superheat
    8–20°F

    Subcooling barely moves with indoor and outdoor conditions on a TXV system, which is why manufacturers give a single value. Aim within 3°F of it.

    Fixed orifice

    Charge to the manufacturer's charging chart or a target-superheat calculator (indoor wet bulb + outdoor dry bulb). Target superheat can range from about 5°F to 40°F. Subcooling varies with load; around 10°F at 95°F outdoor is typical.

    Definitions
    • Total superheat = suction line temp at the service valve − suction saturation temp
    • Evaporator superheat = suction line temp at the evaporator outlet − suction saturation temp
    • Subcooling = condensing saturation temp − liquid line temp at the service valve
    Field tips
    Charging
    • Follow the manufacturer's charging procedure when there is one. Pressure-based methods only work with clean coils and correct airflow.
    • Never judge charge from one side. Read superheat and subcooling.
    • Charge heat pumps in cooling. If you must charge in heating by pressure, coils clean and airflow right, and come back to verify in cooling season.
    • Go slowly on heat pumps and anything with a suction accumulator. Liquid can sit in the accumulator and migrate out later, leaving the system overcharged.
    Not enough load
    • Don't charge with the evaporator saturation below 32°F. As a rule, there isn't enough load below about 50°F indoor wet bulb or 68°F return air dry bulb.
    • If conditions are inside that range and the coil is still too cold, look for air bypassing the coil.
    TXV systems
    • A TXV needs pressure drop across it: about 100 psi on R-22, 160 psi on R-410A. Without it, liquid backs up in the condenser: suction and liquid pressures low, superheat high, subcooling normal to high.
    • On a cool day, restrict condenser airflow to bring head pressure up to a 90–95°F-day equivalent, then check evaporator superheat and subcooling against spec. Not a charging method for charge-by-pressure systems, and keep the coil above freezing.
    • The valve can't control superheat past its bulb. If total superheat is high, insulate the suction line; a suction line over about 55°F deserves a closer look.
    • Before replacing a TXV, check bulb location and mounting. It needs metal straps; tape and zip ties don't hold. Valves themselves rarely fail.
    Other
    • Mixed refrigerant: recover into an empty evacuated cylinder, let it stabilize 12–24 h, then compare its pressure with the saturation pressure at the cylinder's temperature. More than 3–5°F off: evacuate, replace the drier, recharge with new refrigerant. Separate gauges per refrigerant avoid it.
    • Airflow problems also come from condenser air recirculation or poor clearance, and on 90%+ furnaces a blocked secondary heat exchanger.
    • Leave gauges off a sealed system once its charge is verified, unless you suspect a refrigerant problem. Most calls are dirt and maintenance.
    • Verify capacity: BTU/h = 4.5 × CFM × Δh, with Δh the enthalpy change across the coil from wet bulb readings (see the Enthalpy calculator).
