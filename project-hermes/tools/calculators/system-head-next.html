Pump Sizing
Closed hydronic loops · flow · head · check

Works out the flow and head a closed loop needs (the duty point you select a pump against), checks what an existing system is actually moving using the heat source and a thermometer, and, if you have the manufacturer's curve, where the pump will run.

Fluid

Average of supply and return at design. Glycol is % by volume, as mixed or read with a refractometer.

Flow the loop needs

Load the circuit or pump serves. Residential boilers are usually designed around a 20 °F ΔT; check the equipment's spec.

Head the loop needs

Friction through the longest circuit (supply and return), at the flow above. Static height doesn't count in a closed loop.

Fittings

Components from manufacturer data

Boiler heat exchanger, air separator, flow-check, zone valve, coil… Enter pressure drop at this flow (ft or psi) or the Cv.

Check an existing pump by heat

What the system is actually moving. Run the heat source at a steady, known firing rate for 15 minutes or more, then read supply and return at the source.

Use output, not input: rated output from the label, or input × efficiency at the current firing rate. A modulating boiler at low fire gives a much smaller number.

Pump curve optional

Only from the manufacturer's submittal or tech support, for the pump and speed setting actually installed. Enter 3–6 points read off the curve.

Flow (GPM)Head (ft)

Gauges on the pump's suction and discharge ports, same height, same gauge (or zeroed against each other).
