Motor type

Run an ECM without a tester
Use the right tool first. We always recommend appropriate test equipment for checking electrical equipment of any kind. Where an ECM tester fits the motor, use it: ECM testers at TruTech Tools. The method below is a fallback for when no tester is available, and only for constant-torque motors. Line voltage is present during the test; work within your training and qualifications.

First, which ECM is it? Look at the connectors on the motor.

  • Constant torque (Genteq X13 and similar): one terminal block with C L G N and speed taps 1–5. This method applies. Genteq's ECM tester is not designed for these.
  • Variable speed (Genteq 2.0 / 2.3 / Eon, or 3.0 with a 4-pin plug): a separate 5-pin power plug and a 16-pin control plug. This method does not apply. Genteq directs these to its TECINspect tester. Never put line voltage on the 16-pin plug.

Constant-torque motor: run it on one tap with a 9 V battery

  1. Power off at the disconnect and wait 5 minutes before handling connectors (the motor's capacitors need to discharge).
  2. Unplug the low-voltage signal wire(s) from the speed taps.
  3. Find a tap the equipment actually uses (wiring diagram, or where the wire was). A tap with no program won't run the motor; that's normal, so try another.
  4. With insulated clip leads, connect the battery: negative (−) to C, positive (+) to the tap. A fresh 9 V battery is inside the motor's DC signal range of 9–23 V; a weak one may not be. 24 VAC from the unit's transformer works the same way (common to C, R to the tap).
  5. Restore power with hands clear. Line voltage must be at L and N: within ±10% of the motor's rated 115, 230, 277 or 460 V. The X13 is voltage-specific.
  6. The motor should start on that tap within a few seconds. Rocking back and forth at start-up is normal.
  7. Power off and wait before removing the clips. Reconnect the signal wires.

What the result points to (Genteq's sequence)

  • Runs on the battery but not on a system call: the motor responds to a signal, so follow the call back: thermostat, board output and wiring to the tap.
  • Correct line voltage at L–N, signal on a programmed tap, and it still doesn't run: Genteq's guide moves on to motor / control replacement for this model.

Speed taps take low-voltage signal only (24 VAC or 9–23 VDC); line voltage on a tap destroys the control. Source: Genteq (Regal) ECM Service Guide G9971, rev. 4-10-14: Model X13 diagnostics and Figures 9–10; safety warnings on page 1. Other makers' constant-torque motors are similar; check their literature.

Nameplate

Sets the references the readings below are compared with.

Dual-rated nameplates: enter both, e.g. 208-230. A blank service factor is treated as 1.0. MCC (max continuous current) is on the compressor label or spec sheet.

Insulation to ground

Megger each terminal to ground with the motor disconnected.

Over range? Enter the meter's maximum. Phase-to-phase insulation only applies to separated multi-lead windings; on a 3-terminal motor or compressor it measures the winding copper.

Winding resistance

Number the three terminals 1, 2, 3 however you like, then read across each pair. Zero your leads first.

An open winding reads OL and can't be entered. The add-up check confirms the readings and terminals; it can't detect shorted turns. Comparing with the manufacturer's resistance spec can.

Run capacitor

Tolerance is printed on the can (commonly ±5%, ±6% or ±10%). Or check it running: amps through the start winding and volts across the capacitor.
